The physics

Three ground rules the engine never breaks — because a coach is only worth listening to if its numbers are honest.

G

Kinematic G, not sensor G

A phone's recorded lateral-G channel over-reads the real force by about 1.29× at an apex. So RaceHero never coaches from it: every G number in the engine is computed kinematically, from your actual path and speed — v²κ, the geometry of the corner you really drove.

▲

Terrain is ground truth

Coaching targets are graded against real topography: elevation profiles checked against survey-grade DEM data across a catalogue of 8,400+ real tracks, plus all 24 sim tracks. When a brake target moves because the road falls away, that's the ground talking — not a guess.

⚑

The game owns the line

In sim racing, start/finish comes from the game itself — the app never plants its own line and hopes. On real tracks, your own first lap plants it, the way racing actually works: you cross your own path, and timing starts.

Proven in simulation

Every engine change is proven in simulation before it ships — synthetic laps with known ground truth, run through the real app code. If a fix can't beat the harness, it doesn't reach your ears. Here's what that discipline measures.

Where things happened is what sample rate buys. A coach that says "you braked 6 metres late" is making a location claim — and a logger can only locate a brake point as precisely as its fix rate allows. We measured it: one braking zone, 220 km/h into Turn 4 at the Nürburgring GP, re-measured from sampled data alone at three rates.

Brake points located to about a metre. At 10 Hz, the same braking zone wanders by up to eleven.

Measured on a ground-truth reconstruction of the Nürburgring GP driven with a real session's speed profile, sampled at 16 phases per rate; one identical brake detector at every rate, so what remains is pure sampling error. Delta readouts sharpen the same way: sampling noise on a live delta falls from ±39 ms at 10 Hz to ±16 ms at 25 Hz to ±8 ms at 50 Hz. Lap times, honestly, barely change — this was never a timing feature. It's a coaching-precision feature.

A coach that learns you

The physics is universal. The coaching is personal — because the engine calibrates itself to the only driver it coaches: you. All of this runs on your phone, on your data, today.

1

It remembers your laps

The coach carries every corner's last-lap result into the next lap. "Later than last lap — you braked early again" is a comparison against your driving, not a textbook.

2

It learns your points

Your brake points and turn-in points are learned from your own fastest laps — where you actually brake, not where a formula guesses. Your reference lap is built from your best sectors, and targets are set at what you've demonstrably done, never at your one luckiest moment.

3

It explains the why

Every debrief starts with the cause — the input that cost the time, at the corner where it happened — drawn from a 190-entry coaching library built from real coaching sources. Numbers come second. Verdicts before measurements, always.

Learning from the field

The next layer — designed, being built, and not switched on for anyone yet. When it arrives, it works on one rule:

Only validated laps teach the coach. Every lap RaceHero records already carries a validation verdict — a lap that closed short, or from the wrong direction, can never become a reference. The field layer will learn only from laps that passed. And your data stays yours: opt-in only, never bundled; anonymized aggregates, distilled on your device before anything leaves it; no one's raw laps, ever. A coach that gets smarter from the field — without ever seeing your name attached to a lap.
