How she coaches

Every session starts the same way — her contract with you: Lap 1 I show you the track. Lap 2 we speed up. Lap 3 I coach.

1

Lap 1 — she shows you the track

A guided lap at pace. As each corner comes up, Lena walks you through it — where to brake, where to turn in, what the corner asks of the car. No stopwatch pressure yet; just learn the lap.

2

Lap 2 — we speed up

The training wheels come off in stages. Lena steps the pace up and starts watching your inputs — your real throttle and brake — against the lines you just learned.

3

Lap 3 — she coaches

Full coaching, live. Two focus corners per lap, never a flood: the brake point that's drifting, the corner that's costing you, calm words when it goes wrong. Afterwards, the debrief names the one thing to fix next.

How she learns

The Difference & Inference Engine

She doesn't grade your lap against a textbook. She measures the difference between what you did and what your data says was there, infers the one change that matters — and checks the next lap to prove it moved.

The gap
Take a hairpin. Your foot leaves the throttle — and for a beat, nothing. Then the brake. That pause is coasting: at 100 km/h, every tenth of a second between the pedals is nearly three metres of road where the car is neither driven nor slowed. The corner is being given away before you've even turned the wheel.
The pattern
She measures that gap at every braking zone, on every lap — and its siblings: the pause between releasing the brake and picking the throttle back up, the overlap as the pedals trade places. Once is noise. The same gap, turning up in the same slow corners lap after lap, is a habit — and a habit can be coached.
The call
The differences are inferred into one change, in the words you'd hear in the car:
“Into the hairpin, your feet take a breath between the pedals. The car coasts, and the corner's gone before it starts. Close the gap — that's the whole job there.”
The proof
Then she watches the next lap. If the gap closed, the difference shrinks — and if it didn't, she says so, and you go again. Nothing is coached on a hunch: every lesson has to survive your data.

A coachable moment is one she can measure, point to, and watch improve. That's coachability, measured.
