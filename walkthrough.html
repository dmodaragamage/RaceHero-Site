When a channel goes quiet

Mid-session, the connection card names it in a sentence — never a silent freeze, never a percentage:

Your pedals went quiet — check RaceHero Link is still running on your PC.
The game feed stopped — check the game is still running and sending telemetry.
GPS went quiet — it comes back on its own with a clear view of the sky.
Lap timing went quiet — keep driving; it re-locks at the start/finish line.

And if the whole feed stops moving, the live-data strip says so in red: Stopped — the feed went quiet.

Twenty seconds into a session with no data at all, the coach says it out loud, once: "I don't have your data yet. Check the PC Link shows connected — I'll start coaching the moment it arrives." These states weren't in the first walkthrough — it only ever showed everything working.

You started careful — 2:11, 2:09 — like a driver learning a new place properly. Then something clicked and the time fell away: 2:08, 2:07, 2:07.1. That wasn't luck. Your braking got later everywhere at once, and your hands got quiet. That's real driving improving, in one session.

THE LAP, CORNER BY CORNER — WHAT YOU DID

Turn 1 — on your best lap you clipped it perfectly, 70 km/h at the slowest point, same as your own best there. Nothing to change. Scrub it →

Turn 3 — the slowest turn on the track, and your most consistent: the same speed, every single lap. Whatever you're doing with your eyes there, do it everywhere.

Turn 4 — the one that matters. You hit the apex every time — the line is right — but you brake about a car-length too early and roll in at 85. Your own best entry here says the car can take more. Scrub it →

Turn 6 — you run a touch wide, and it makes the next part arrive before you're ready. A car-length earlier turn-in fixes it. Scrub it →

Turn 7 — flat out, every lap, top of the speed trace. It sets up your run to the line. Keep doing exactly this.

Corners with nothing true to say get no sentence — the coach never pads. Every number above comes from this session's own data.

THE MOMENT THAT MATTERED

Last lap, Turn 1: you went hunting for a 2:06 and the back stepped out — your speed there fell to 40, where your best lap carries 70. I'm not counting that lap against you — I've set it aside in your record, fairly, the way a steward would. But let's be honest about the lesson: the spin wasn't bad luck, it was the bill for braking and turning at the same time. Next time, pick one.
