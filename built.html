The story

How Lena was built.

Anyone can put a voice in your ear. We set out to build a coach — one who tells the truth, learns from every lap, and is actually there with you in the car. This is how she was made.

Coaches are made, not prompted.

Start with the truth ↓ Meet her first
Act one · Truth first

Built on ground that doesn't move.

Before she was a voice, she was a discipline: never say what the data doesn't support.

Lena stands on RaceHero's physics engine — real tracks, mapped with real terrain, every climb, drop and camber in place. Under every word she says sits your real data: your line, your speed, your pedals, the corners as you actually drove them.

That gives her the first rule she was built with, and she has never been allowed to break it: she may not say what your data doesn't support. Ask her something your laps can't answer and she won't improvise a confident-sounding guess — she'll tell you she can't answer, and why.

A coach who guesses is just a stopwatch with opinions. She was built to be the other thing.

Act two · She goes to school

Her lessons get graded too.

You thought the driver was the only one being coached.

Every session Lena coaches is also a session she is marked on. Her calls are replayed against the recorded laps — what she said, when she said it, and what your data already knew at that moment. When she gets something wrong, it gets found, and it gets corrected. When the data shows her something she was blind to — a signal in your driving she wasn't watching — she gets taught it, and it joins what she looks for from then on.

We call the loop the Difference & Inference Engine: measure the difference between what she said and what your data knew, infer the one change that closes it, then check the next lap to see whether it moved. Coachability, measured — hers, as rigorously as yours.

She doesn't arrive finished. She arrives honest, and she goes to school after every session you drive.

Act three · One person, on purpose

Presence is a design decision.

A voice is easy. A person in the car with you — that had to be researched, then built.

Before Lena ever turned a lap, we studied what makes a voice in your ear feel like someone who is actually there: the psychology of presence, the way rally co-drivers talk a driver down a road they've never seen, the callout discipline that keeps a cockpit calm when everything is happening at once. Different fields, one conclusion — presence isn't a tone of voice. It's a set of promises, kept every lap. So we built the promises in as rules:

Rule one

One voice — hers

Every word you hear is Lena: same voice, same person, lap after lap, session after session. Never a stranger's voice swapped in when a connection stumbles.

Rule two

Talk on the straights, strikes in the corners

Teaching belongs where your attention is free. In the corner itself, you get the short calls a co-driver gives — or silence. Nothing she says should ever cost you a corner.

Rule three

If she loses her connection, she says so

In her own voice, the moment it happens — not a card on a screen you're not looking at. And when she's back, she says that too. You always know whether she's with you.

Rule four

She remembers you

Your brake points, your habits, the corner that bit you last session. She carries your history into the car, because a coach who has to be re-introduced every lap isn't a coach.

The research had a name, too — the Immersion Engine: the layer whose whole job is that she doesn't feel delivered. She feels present.

Act four · She rides with you

The build ends. The riding begins.

That's how she was built: on truth she isn't allowed to bend, in a school she never graduates from, as one person on purpose. But a coach isn't finished when she's built — she's finished never. She gets in the car with you, learns the way you drive, session after session: where you brake, what you do when a corner goes wrong, the one thing to fix next.

She was built to be in your corner. Then she climbs into your car to prove it.
